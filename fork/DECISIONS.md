# Design decisions

Agreed with the user. These override anything in PLAN.md that says otherwise. Change a
decision here first, with the date, before changing the code. Take the next free number for a
new one.

## Base and scope

Agreed 2026-10-17.

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| D1 | **Base: OpenNutriTracker.** Compared with SparkyFitness (needs its own Express/Postgres server), Fud AI (no food-database search; whole diary in one preferences entry), NutriTrace, Waistline, FoodYou and FitBook. ONT has search, barcode, recipes and AI text/photo logging. Data stays on the phone in encrypted Hive, and it has an active community. |
| D2 | **Android only, no server of our own.** Third-party services the app already calls (Open Food Facts, ONT's food backend, the user's own AI provider) are fine. |
| D3 | **Must-have logging methods:** barcode, search, recipes, AI text describe, AI photo, AI photo with text. **Not wanted:** voice logging (the keyboard's dictation covers it). |
| D4 | **Bring your own AI**, through ONT's existing providers (Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, any OpenAI-compatible server). |
| D5 | **Testing on the Android emulator**, the `xperia1vi_api36` AVD shared with openGym-Hevier (`fork/tools/emulator.sh`). |

## First answers

Agreed 2026-10-17 (PLAN.md §6, Q1–Q3 and Q5).

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| D7 | **A cheap vision model; the database supplies the numbers.** *Revised 2026-10-18.* Starting defaults: **Claude Haiku and `gpt-5.6-luna`**, both from vendors upstream already screened. Haiku 4.5 (upstream's direct Anthropic model) today; Haiku 5.5 ($0.10/$0.50 per M tokens, about $0.0003 a photo, cheaper than luna) is added as a fork catalogue entry, pinned to Anthropic, once it passes the photo test. Cost no longer decides: every cheap model is a few cents a month at personal volume. The photo test (`fork/eval/`) decides which leads. *Moved last 2026-10-22* (PLAN.md phase 4): any capable model does the job and users bring their own, so it only picks the suggested default. Chinese open-weight models run as challengers in the same test, served from non-Chinese hosts: GLM-5.3-flash on a US fp8 host, DeepSeek V4.1 flash on Nebius (EU), plus Qwen 3.8 flash (Alibaba, US region). Keep upstream's grounding: the model names foods and counts, nutrition comes from the database, and the photo path never estimates grams (upstream #250). |
| D8 | **Own app id and name: OpenBite**, installed beside the official app (openGym-Hevier's S7 approach: a fork-owned Gradle file applied by one seam line, S20). *Named 2026-10-23*: OpenBite (open source, and "open bite"), chosen over OpenFork, OpenFridge and Forkcast; no Play Store app by that name, openbite.dev unregistered, openbite.app taken; trademark registers not yet searched. App id `io.github.lodepav.openbite`, a namespace the fork owns (the GitHub account) rather than a domain it doesn't. Only the launcher name changes so far; the app's own text still says OpenNutriTracker. |
| D9 | **Break a dish down before estimating.** When the model names a dish the database can't match, it breaks the dish into ingredients with gram amounts (D12), and each ingredient is matched as usual. Only an ingredient that still matches nothing gets the model's own estimate. It is clearly marked as an AI estimate and is editable before logging. Fork-only behind a seam, because upstream deliberately forbids model-supplied numbers (`meal_items_api.dart`). Revised 2026-10-18: ingredients first, estimates last. |
| D10 | **Google Drive backup through a sync app; the app only writes backup files** (openGym-Hevier's D20). *Detailed 2026-10-18.* Unlike openGym, upstream ONT has no automatic backup, only a manual export with a save dialog each time, so the fork adds one: Settings → Auto-backup writes upstream's JSON export zip into a folder the user picks once with the system picker (Storage Access Framework, persisted grant), one dated file a day, the newest 14 kept. It writes when the app goes to the background and something changed since the last file, so no background job and no Android background limits. A sync app (Autosync for Google Drive, FolderSync, a Syncthing fork) mirrors the folder. Restore is upstream's Import. No Drive client, no Google sign-in. Offered upstream as #1356. |

## Food data

Agreed 2026-10-17 (PLAN.md §6, Q4).

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| D11 | **Generic foods come from open tables bundled into the app as an offline asset. No API key and no backend.** **USDA FoodData Central** (SR Legacy, Foundation, FNDDS survey; public domain; household portions such as "1 medium" or "1 slice") and **BLS 4.0** (Germany, Max Rubner-Institut, CC BY 4.0, ~7,100 foods, close to Latvian staples: rye bread, quark, kefir, herring, sauerkraut). Every row keeps its source. Packaged products keep coming from Open Food Facts live, as upstream does. `fork/tools/build_food_db.py` builds the asset. No more tables: dishes come from recipes (D12), and their ingredients are covered by these two. A few dishes the menus serve and the tables lack (solyanka, cepelinai) are bundled as rows the builder works out from recipes over these tables (`fork/data/dishes.json`, source `fork_dishes`, 2026-10-24). Fineli and Livsmedelsverket are dropped (2026-10-18). Not usable: NEVO (unchanged copies only) and NutriData (no stated licence). |
| D12 | **Dishes come from recipes, and the AI writes the recipe.** No open recipe database covers Latvian or Georgian food with gram amounts (researched 2026-10-18; the large scraped datasets are research-only or of doubtful licence). So: (1) for a dish the tables lack, the model returns its ingredients with grams, naming the nearest generic ingredient where a local one is missing (grey peas → dried split peas, sulguni → brined mozzarella-type cheese); (2) the ingredients are matched in the tables (D11), so the numbers stay grounded; (3) the breakdown can be saved as a recipe with upstream's recipe builder, which makes the dish instant and offline next time and lets the user adjust it to how they cook it; (4) four CC0 Latvian recipes with gram amounts (grey peas with bacon, pelmeni, sklandu rauši, layered rye-bread dessert; *The Cuisine of Latvia*, Latvian Institute, data.gov.lv) ship as starter recipes. Agreed 2026-10-18. |

## Logging

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| D14 | **Leftovers are subtracted from the logged meal, manually or by AI.** Agreed 2026-10-21. Manual: a "Leftovers" action on an entry or a group of entries logged together, as an amount or a fraction left. AI: a photo of what was left, sent with the group's current entries and, if still cached, the original photo (kept 24 hours in app-private storage, never backed up). The model returns only a count or fraction left per entry, never nutrients, so the numbers stay the logged entries'. Its answer is marked as an AI estimate and reviewed before saving. Estimating a fraction from a photo is a visual quantity estimate, which upstream's photo path avoids (#250), so the AI part is fork-only behind a seam; the manual part goes upstream. |

## Targets

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| D13 | **MacroFactor-style algorithms sit beside upstream's, chosen by a switch.** Two independent settings: *Weight trend* — 7-day average (upstream) or smoothed trend; *Calorie target* — formula (upstream, unchanged) or adaptive. Adaptive follows MacroFactor's published structure: linearly interpolated weigh-ins, an exponentially smoothed trend, change over 20 days, stored energy from fat 9,400 / lean 1,800 kcal/kg with the fat share from Forbes via Hall 2008 (flat 6,000 kcal/kg as fallback), expenditure blended from upstream's IOM 2005 prior × (1 − 4 × goal rate), no update in a week with under 4 logged days or no weigh-in, and an adherence-neutral weekly check-in with a capped change the user accepts or edits. Upstream's taper and low-calorie warning stay; logged activities are not added to an adaptive target (they are already in measured expenditure). Smoothing, blend and cap weights are ours, chosen by simulation against known expenditure. Research: [research/phase3-macrofactor-algorithms.md](research/phase3-macrofactor-algorithms.md). |
| D15 | **Build everything now; wait for nobody** (2026-10-23, the user). Every planned feature is in the app by the end of the week (2026-10-25). **Upstream's:** a feature on upstream `develop`, in an open PR or on its roadmap is cherry-picked or built now, not waited for, and swapped for upstream's version when a release carries it (SEAMS.md "Carried upstream changes" lists what to drop at each merge). **Built elsewhere** (another fork, another app): its implementation is taken and configured the fork's way, but only after a review: is it sound, does it follow upstream's and the fork's standards (README rules, AGENTS.md), can it be simpler. The review's findings are written down (in PLAN.md beside the item) and fixed in the port, before it is merged. Replaces §0b's "port or wait" rule. Upstream PRs still wait for the live tests (phase 4). |
| D16 | **Releases on GitHub, with an in-app updater** (2026-10-24, the user), after openGym-Hevier's. OpenBite has its own semver in `fork/VERSION`, from 0.1.0; Android's versionName is it and its versionCode is major·10000 + minor·100 + patch (`android/app/fork.gradle`); pubspec keeps upstream's version, and About says "0.1.0 · based on OpenNutriTracker 2.5.0". `fork/tools/release-apk.sh` numbers a release from the merges since the last (`fork/tools/release_notes.dart`: a merge adding a decision raises the minor, anything else the patch, a major only when asked), writes `fork/CHANGELOG.md`, builds the full flavour unsigned, signs it with Go Hevier's release key (shared: one key, one pin, `~/.config/hevier/`), runs `fork/tools/release-check.sh`, tags `openbite-vX.Y.Z` and publishes a GitHub release on `lodepav/OpenNutriTracker` with the APK and its `.sha256`. The release build checks that repository's latest release once per session (`lib/fork/update/`), offers a newer one once per version, downloads it and has Android's installer open it after a SHA-256 check (S76). Debug, profile and develop builds never check. |
| D17 | **Expenditure and check-in settings** (2026-10-24, the user). One screen, "Expenditure and check-ins", reached from the adaptive target, in three groups. *Estimate*: responsiveness (Steady / Standard / Responsive, which only differ in blend weight; [research/phase3-simulation.md](research/phase3-simulation.md), "Responsiveness"), the 28-day cycle window, and the days left out. A Diary day's "Leave out of expenditure" switch drops its intake from the estimate and keeps its weigh-in, for a day logged only in part. *Activity*: Off (D13 as before) / Weekly / Each day, counting steps from Health Connect at the net cost of walking (ACSM; step length 0.414 × height) and logged workouts. Imported workouts count at the kcal the device reported, not upstream's credited share; steps inside an imported workout are not counted twice. Workouts count at a share the user sets, 30–100%, by default upstream's body-composition suggestion (Careau 2021). The estimate takes the window's mean counted activity off, so activity that is overstated hardly moves the average target. Weekly adds the past week's mean at each check-in, outside the cap; Each day adds the day's own to its goal (S12), with Home keeping today's stored goal in step (S13). *Check-in*: the weekday (or a week after the last), the largest change (100 / 200 / 300 kcal or none), and automatic acceptance. |
| D18 | **Check-ins after a real journey** (2026-10-24, the user). A 17-month MacroFactor journey replayed through the check-in (`fork/tools/replay_journey.dart`; the data stays outside the repo) showed the estimate close to MacroFactor's, and three faults. (1) A changed goal reached the target only at the next check-in and then at most 200 kcal a week, up to two weeks late; in all those months the cap never once held back a settled estimate's odd week. Now the cap holds the *estimate* under the target (a record keeps it as `baseKcal`) and the goal's energy applies in full, and a record keeps the goal it was set for, so a changed pace or target weight makes a check-in due at once ("Your goal changed" on Home). (2) The first weeks held a formula prior 500 kcal too high, the cap letting the target down 200 kcal a week: no cap before the first check-in or while the estimate calibrates. (3) After weeks unlogged the window priced a weight change while not logging at the logged days' intake: an update now needs 60% of the window logged (`minLoggedShare`), and calibration counts updates rather than days, so weigh-ins before logging began do not use it up. Synthetic users unchanged (error 73–74). The kcal per kg of a goal stays Forbes's, not MacroFactor's 7,700: the data cannot tell which fits. | accepted |
| D19 | **Activity and start-up after the journey runs** (2026-10-24, the user). Runs with activity on the same journey, and the start of the one without, found four things. (1) Steps switched on with history before them counted walking twice for about four weeks: the estimate before held it, and only the blend let it out while the target added it back. The estimator now takes the first two weeks' mean activity off once, on the day counted activity starts (the prior's own subtraction on day 0 is the same case). (2) The daily mode added any day's activity in full; one watch workout raised a day by over 1,000 kcal. A day now adds at most 800 kcal (`maxDailyActivityKcal`), and the goal note says how much of today's goal is activity. (3) The weekly mode's activity moves the target outside the cap; the check-in says by how much against the last one. (4) The first update waited for 60% of a 20-day window logged, the weigh-ins before logging counted as a gap: the window now reaches back no further than the first logged day, and the warm-up, 7 days from 14, counts from it. The first estimate came a week sooner and the largest weekly move fell from 278 to 185 kcal; synthetic error 74 → 76. A switch counts every kg as 7,700 kcal, as most apps do (off by default): on the journey it cut the drift against MacroFactor's targets from 7.0 to 4.3 kg. The presets stay: a reaction-speed study had ours ahead of MacroFactor in the user's slowest episode (Responsive 80% in 67 days, MacroFactor not in 120), and a faster Standard bought little speed for its wobble. | accepted |
